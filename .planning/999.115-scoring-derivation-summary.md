# How the QU.I.R.K. readiness score is derived — scoring v3

**Prepared 2026-09-14 for the Friday discussion. Status: the model is landed and green on
`scoring-999.115-model-fix`; it is not yet merged to `main`.**

**How to read this document.** Part 1 is written to be said out loud to a client. Parts 2-4 are
presenter notes — the arithmetic, the free parameters, and the limits — and are not client-safe as
written, because they name exactly where the model is a first fit. No framing decision was made
before this was drafted, so it is layered rather than split: take Part 1 to a client, keep Parts
2-4 for the room.

---

## Part 1 — The narrative

### What the score is

A single 0-100 number answering one question: **how exposed is this estate to the arrival of
cryptographically relevant quantum computing, and how much of that exposure is within reach of
remediation today?**

It is built from six domains, each scored out of 25:

| Domain | What it measures |
|---|---|
| Hygiene | Plaintext services, services on the wrong ports, scan reachability |
| Modern TLS | Protocol versions, legacy suites, unknown open services |
| Identity & Trust | Certificate expiry and provenance, Kerberos/SAML/DNSSEC signing strength, mTLS |
| Crypto Agility | Key-type diversity, RSA dependence, post-quantum key exchange |
| Data at Rest | Database, storage, Kubernetes and vault encryption posture |
| Data in Motion | Email and message-broker transport security |

**Domains we did not assess are excluded, not assumed.** If a scan never reached your message
brokers, Data in Motion shows an em-dash and the headline is computed from the five domains we did
see, with the coverage stated on the report. An unassessed domain neither helps nor hurts the
score. This matters because the opposite behaviour — awarding full marks to anything unmeasured —
is a defect this product has had, twice, and fixed.

### What changed in v3, and why the number went down

v3 changes what the number *means*. Two corrections drive it.

**1. Consequence is now absolute, not proportional.**

The old model priced every weakness as a *proportion* of the estate. Five expired certificates in a
17-service environment scored worse than the same five in a 500-service one. That is not how risk
works: an attacker needs one. In the old model a genuinely catastrophic estate could be diluted by
everything around it that happened to be fine, and the strongest statement the product could make
about it was a score in the high 80s wearing the word "FAIR" — the digits saying one thing and the
label another.

Now, the *count* of open CRITICAL and HIGH findings sets a **ceiling** on the score, regardless of
how small a fraction of the estate they represent. The label is then derived from the number, so
the two can no longer disagree.

**2. A perfect classical score no longer reaches 100.**

An estate can be immaculate by every classical measure — current TLS, clean certificates, no
plaintext — and still have no post-quantum key exchange anywhere. It is exactly as exposed to
harvest-now-decrypt-later as it was before the engagement began. On a *quantum* readiness
assessment, 100 has to mean quantum-ready. An estate with no observed hybrid post-quantum key
exchange is capped at the top of GOOD.

### What a client sees

- **A capped score always says so.** The report never presents a capped number as a computed one.
  It names the finding set that set the ceiling and discloses what the score would otherwise have
  been: *"5 open CRITICAL findings — score limited to 18 (computed 71)."*
- **A capped estate is not a frozen one.** The ceiling compresses the score into the permitted
  range rather than flattening it. Remediation still moves the number, and the roadmap's
  per-item point estimates still mean something. This was not free — see Part 3.
- **Scores from different scoring versions are not comparable.** Every report carries a version
  marker (`scoring v3 — not comparable with v2 or earlier scores`). Historical scores are not
  rewritten; the discontinuity is disclosed rather than hidden.

### The one-sentence version

*The old score measured what proportion of your estate was affected, so a serious problem could be
diluted by everything around it that was fine. It now measures consequence directly, because an
attacker only needs the one — and where a cap applies, the report tells you which finding set it
and what the number would otherwise have been.*

## Part 2 — The derivation

### The method, and why it matters more than the result

**The model was chosen by measurement against a calibration ladder, not by argument.**

That is a deliberate reaction to a failure. A previous attempt at this (Phase 188) failed not
because the ideas were wrong but because **nothing could tell whether a change had helped.**
Roughly 3,200 lines of scoring tests were passing the entire time the score was blind — they
asserted mechanism, bounds and rendering, never *meaning*. One of them was satisfied perfectly by a
function returning a constant.

So the first deliverable was an instrument, not a model: a property suite (`P1`-`P8`) plus a
five-rung calibration ladder. Each property is a statement a client would assume without being
told. Each ladder rung is a reference estate described **in infrastructure terms only** — host
counts, expired-certificate ratios, finding severities — with its target band **set blind by the
operator before any rung was scored.** That blindness is what makes the ladder a measurement rather
than a description of whatever the model already did.

Candidate models were then scored against it and the winner read off.

### The ladder, measured 2026-09-14

| Rung | Reference estate | Target band (set blind) | v2 | v3 | Cap disclosed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Pristine, PQC-ready | EXCELLENT | 100 | **100** | none |
| R2 | Well-run, no PQC | GOOD | 95 | **78** | no post-quantum key exchange observed (computed 93) |
| R3 | Typical enterprise | FAIR | 91 | **46** | 3 open HIGH findings (computed 85) |
| R4 | Neglected | POOR | 85 | **24** | 2 open CRITICAL findings (computed 72) |
| R5 | 31-host purpose-built vulnerable lab | score < 30 | 87 | **18** | 5 open CRITICAL findings (computed 71) |

**5/5.** Under v2, four of these five estates — including one with 5 CRITICAL findings, 14 HIGH,
and 29% of its certificates expired — earned the product's top grade.

### The six changes

| # | Change | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Absolute consequence ceiling** | The count of open CRITICAL/HIGH findings caps the score *before* the band is derived, so number and label agree |
| 2 | **PQC ceiling ("no PQC, no 100")** | Zero observed hybrid key exchange caps at the top of GOOD |
| 3 | **Prevalence curve** | `ratio ** 0.5` — a small-but-real prevalence stops reading as proportionally trivial |
| 4 | **Undetermined key types penalised** | Certificates whose key type could not be determined no longer score as best-case |
| 5 | **`agility_high_impact_ratio` removed** | A finding *count* is not a population badness is proportional to |
| 6 | **Driver/headline agreement** | The report can no longer cite a domain the headline excluded as unassessed |

### The parameters, and how many are actually free

Current model: **41 weighted terms, summing to 297.0.** Bands: EXCELLENT ≥ 85, GOOD ≥ 70,
MODERATE ≥ 55, FAIR ≥ 35, POOR below.

The ceilings are **derived from the published band table, not chosen**:

| Trigger | Ceiling | Derivation |
|---|---|---|
| ≥ 5 open CRITICAL | 25 | `DEEP_CRITICAL_CEILING` — **the one free parameter** |
| ≥ 1 open CRITICAL | 34 | top of POOR = `FAIR − 1` |
| ≥ 3 open HIGH | 54 | top of FAIR = `MODERATE − 1` |
| ≥ 1 open HIGH | 69 | top of MODERATE = `GOOD − 1` |
| No PQC observed | 84 | top of GOOD = `EXCELLENT − 1` |

Reading them through the band table rather than hardcoding collapses the free-parameter count from
four to **one**, and means a future band rebalance carries the ceilings with it automatically. The
single remaining constant, 25, comes from the operator's R5 rung.

**Why PQC is a ceiling and not a bigger bonus.** This was forced by measurement, not preferred. A
PQC bonus already existed at 8.0 — the joint-largest in the table — and adopting hybrid
X25519MLKEM768 on *every* endpoint moved the score by **exactly zero points**, because each domain
is clamped at 25 and a clean estate is already there. Any bonus is absorbed the same way. A ceiling
is the only shape that survives the clamp.

### Compression, not clamping — the load-bearing detail

The ceiling **compresses** the score into `[0, ceiling]`; it does not clamp to it. This is the
single most important implementation decision here and it was found by measurement, twice.

- A hard `min(score, ceiling)` was implemented first. It made the score **inert** on capped
  estates: renewing every expired certificate on the reference estate moved it **+0**, eliminating
  all plaintext HTTP **+0**, and the strict/balanced/lenient profiles collapsed to one identical
  number. The remediation roadmap would have shown "+0 points" against every recommendation the
  product makes.
- Compressing only *above* the ceiling was tried next. That puts a **cliff at the ceiling**: a
  computed 84 emitted 84, a computed 85 emitted 71, and nothing in the whole range 85-100 ever
  emitted more than 84 — every well-run estate scored below a mediocre one.

Compression across the whole range bounds the maximum just as firmly while preserving ordering,
remediation lift and profile sensitivity. Current measurements on R5: lift **+1** per certificate
renewal, **+2** for eliminating plaintext; profiles **16 / 18 / 19**.

## Part 3 — Known limits, stated plainly

These are the answers to "where is this weakest?", and they should be given before they are asked.

**1. Four of the five ladder rungs are synthetic.** The *shape* — that consequence must be absolute
rather than proportional — is well evidenced. The *constants* are a first fit against one real
estate and four constructed ones. Real-scan rungs should be added before the thresholds are treated
as settled.

**2. Lift granularity shrinks inside the capped region.** Compression preserves ordering, but a
4-point raw gain can read as +1 on a heavily capped estate. Remediation still moves the number; it
moves it in smaller visible increments than the underlying improvement.

**3. Dilution by scan depth is NOT fixed.** This is the honest headline limitation. Observing more
*healthy* endpoints still raises the computed score even when every weakness count is identical:
measured 71 → 74 → 78 → 82 as healthy assessable endpoints are added to the reference estate. The
consequence ceiling *masks* most of this in the emitted number (18 → 18 → 20 → 20), which is
precisely why it needs saying out loud — it reads like a fix and is not one. It will surface at
full size on any estate the ceiling does not bind, i.e. the healthier clients. Root cause is a
denominator (`assessable_endpoint_count`) that grows with scan depth. Tracked, with a proposed
approach, at `.planning/todos/pending/p2b-healthy-endpoints-dilute-the-readiness-score.md`.

**4. The score compares only within a scoring version.** By design, and disclosed on every report.

**5. One earlier claim in this work was wrong and is corrected here.** A candidate simulation
reported 27/27 properties satisfied. It was run under the clamp, and an inert score trivially
satisfies any property asserting the score does not rise. The corrected result is what Parts 2-3
report. This is recorded rather than quietly dropped because it is the same failure mode the whole
instrument exists to catch.

## Part 4 — Questions to expect

**"Did our security get worse?"**
No. The measurement changed, not the estate. The old number was diluting real problems against
everything around them that was fine. Re-scan or re-score if you need a like-for-like current
figure; do not compare a v3 number to a v2 one.

**"Why can't we score 100?"**
You can — with post-quantum key exchange deployed. Without it, a perfect classical score on a
quantum-readiness assessment would tell you your post-quantum posture was complete when it has not
begun.

**"Our score barely moved after we fixed things."**
If a ceiling is binding, improvements below it show in smaller increments. The report names which
finding set is capping you — clearing that set lifts the cap, and that is usually the largest
single move available.

**"Is the number defensible?"**
The mechanism is fully derived and documented, and the thresholds come from the published band
table rather than being chosen. The *calibration* is a first fit against five reference estates,
four of them constructed. That is stated in Part 3 rather than defended.
