# Decision: readiness-score ratio denominator semantics (999.113)

**Status:** DECIDED 2026-09-13
**Decider:** operator (demo-readiness pivot, 2026-09-13)
**Supersedes:** nothing. **Completes:** 999.95 / Phase 188 SCORE-06, which this record finds incomplete.
**Blocks:** the 2026-09-18 client demo — the readiness score is the product's headline output and is
currently blind to real weaknesses.

---

## The question

`quirk/intelligence/scoring.py` computes 35 ratio penalties. 34 of them divide by
`denom = totals.endpoints` — the **probe count** (hosts x probed ports), including ports where
nothing was found. Exactly one, `agility_high_impact_ratio` (line 304), divides by `max(findings, 1)`.

One of those two readings has to be wrong. This decides which.

## Decision

**D1 — Every ratio divides by the population its own numerator is drawn from.**

This is not a new principle. Line 304 already follows it: a ratio of *findings* divides by
*findings*. The defect is that the other 34 sites do not. `_ratio(expired_count, endpoints)` asks
"what share of our PROBES were expired certificates", which is not a meaningful question — probes
are not certificates.

Denominator per family:

| Family | Numerator counts | Denominator | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Certificate | certificates (expired, expiring, self-signed) | `certificate_observations.certs_observed` | `evidence.py:485` |
| Endpoint | endpoints (plaintext HTTP, HTTP-on-TLS, legacy TLS, unknown services, mTLS) | `assessable_endpoint_count` | `evidence.py:479` |
| Findings | findings (high-impact) | non-INFO finding count (`finding_severity_counts`) — **corrected by D1(d), was wrongly believed already-correct at `totals.findings`** | line 304 |
| Domain / connector | domain-specific weaknesses (DAR db/storage/k8s/vault, motion email/broker, agility jwt/openapi/codesign/fuzz, identity kerberos/saml/dnssec/smime/adcs) | `assessable_endpoint_count` — **an approximation, see D3** | — |

**D2 — `assessable_endpoint_count`, not `totals.endpoints`, is the endpoint population.**
This is not a new judgement either; it is enforcing one already written down.
`_endpoints_assessed`'s own docstring (`scoring.py:181`) instructs: *"Callers must pass the
ADVISORY/CLOSED-excluded count (`assessable_endpoint_count`), not `totals.endpoints`"*. That
instruction was honoured for the `endpoints_assessed` predicate and ignored four lines away for
`denom`. We are making the code consistent with its own stated contract.

**D1(d) — the findings family also had a probe-scaling denominator, found during implementation
by arithmetic, not by inspection.** This decision originally read `agility_high_impact_ratio`
(line 304) as "the one site that was always correct" and forbade touching it. That was wrong.
It divides high-impact (HIGH+CRITICAL) findings by `max(totals.findings, 1)` -- and
`totals.findings` INCLUDES INFO findings, which scale with scan depth the same way
`totals.endpoints` does. On the 31-host estate: 19 HIGH+CRITICAL findings against 398 total
findings (330 of them INFO) register as 4.8% (`-0.67` of the 14.0 weight); against the 68
non-INFO (actionable) findings the same 19 register as 27.9% (`-3.91`). This is the same defect
class D1 exists to fix, expressed through a different population. The correct denominator is the
non-INFO (CRITICAL+HIGH+MEDIUM+LOW) finding count, derived from `finding_severity_counts`
(already read by `compute_readiness_score`), guarded the same way every other family denominator
is guarded: an all-INFO or empty scan yields denominator 0, and `_ratio()` returns 0.0 rather
than fabricating a penalty.

| Findings | high-impact findings | non-INFO finding count | `finding_severity_counts` (CRITICAL+HIGH+MEDIUM+LOW) |

**D3 — Domain/connector ratios move to `assessable_endpoint_count` as a STRICT IMPROVEMENT, not a
correct answer.** Their honest population would be "how many databases / brokers / mail servers were
observed", and those counts do not exist in `evidence`. Moving them off the probe count removes the
deep-scan-scores-better defect for them; it does not make their denominator right. **This is
recorded as knowingly incomplete and must be filed as a follow-up todo**, not quietly treated as
solved. Do not let D3 be read later as "the denominators are all correct now."

**D4 — The 25-point clamp interaction must be MEASURED, not reasoned about.**
`_apply_weighted_impacts` starts each category at 25.0 and clamps to `[0, 25]`. Denominators shrink
by roughly 10x on the multihost estate (370 -> 38; 370 -> 17 for certs), so penalties grow by
roughly 10x and categories will floor at 0/25 far more often. **A domain flooring at 0/25 on a
deliberately vulnerable estate is arguably correct. A domain flooring at 0/25 on an ordinary,
mostly-healthy estate is a new defect replacing the old one.** Both cases must be measured before
this ships — see the acceptance criteria below.

**D5 — Do not tune to a target.** The lab score must move because the denominator is now
meaningful, not because someone picked a number that demos well. If the honest result is an
awkward score, the honest result ships. This is restated from the todo because the change is being
made four days before a demo and that is exactly when the temptation exists.

## Why this reading, and not "share of estate"

The todo fairly notes the competing reading: a ratio over all probed surface is a defensible
"share of estate" metric. It is rejected on three grounds, each evidenced:

1. **It makes the score respond to scan configuration rather than to infrastructure.** Same estate,
   `ports_tls` widened from 2 to 10: `totals.endpoints` 54 -> 219 and the score *rose* 89 -> 91.
   A readiness score that improves because you looked harder is not measuring readiness.
2. **It produces an indefensible headline.** 5 of 17 certificates expired — 29% of the estate — and
   Identity scored **25/25**, because `-(5/370)*14.0 = -0.19` rounds away. Over `certs_observed` the
   same evidence is `-(5/17)*14.0 = -4.12` and Identity lands near 19.
3. **The codebase already disagrees with it** at line 304, and `_endpoints_assessed`'s docstring
   already disagrees with it in prose. "Share of estate" is not an existing intentional design; it
   is an unexamined default that one site escaped.

## Acceptance criteria (all four required before this ships)

1. **Direction, on bad infrastructure.** Re-scoring the recorded multihost evidence
   (`endpoints=370`, `assessable=38`, `certs_observed=17`, 5 CRITICAL / 14 HIGH / 33 MEDIUM,
   5 expired + 3 self-signed + 1 expiring of 17 certs) moves the score materially DOWN from 91 and
   moves Identity off 25/25. If it does not, the change did not work — report that, do not adjust
   weights to force it.
2. **No false floor, on ordinary infrastructure.** Re-score at least one ordinary/healthy evidence
   fixture from the existing test corpus. Report every category that lands at 0/25. A healthy estate
   flooring a domain is a BLOCKER — stop and report, do not ship it.
3. **Red-proof.** Prove the new denominator is load-bearing: with the change in place, an
   evidence dict whose only difference is a larger `totals.endpoints` must produce an IDENTICAL
   score (the old code produced a different, higher one). That is the defect, stated as a test.
4. **Golden fixtures regenerated, not edited.** CBOM fixtures and `score-strings.json` are
   generator-drift-gated. Regenerate via their documented commands (CLAUDE.md's generator-drift
   table); never hand-edit a fixture to match a new number.

## Blast radius — stated plainly

**This changes every score the product has ever emitted.** Historical scans re-scored under the new
denominator will not match their stored values. That is intended and is the point of the fix, but it
means: any customer-facing score already delivered was computed under the old semantics, and
`CHANGELOG.md` must say so in plain language rather than burying it as "scoring improvements".
